SafeNow is a worldwide emergency app. It is politically neutral, and its aim is to help people survive, not to make money.

This version is free and does not take payment. The compass, Before / During / After guides, first aid, and checklists stay on the phone. It is available in 20 languages.

What is on the phone

Guides, places you save, and tools stay on the device. There is no account and no SafeNow server for your notes.

Shelter compass

A large arrow points toward a place you saved, with the distance and a walking time. It uses this phone’s location and motion sensors on the device. A street map is not required.

Before, during, and after

Emergency guides are split into short stages: what to prepare, what to do while it is happening, and what to do after. Follow official instructions if they differ from the app.

Stay or leave

Short decision pages help you think through whether to stay or leave in a given situation. Local officials override this guidance.

First aid, including CPR

Short topics such as CPR, bleeding, burns, and choking. Action steps stay in English so a translation cannot change them. This is reference guidance, not a substitute for training or official care.

SOS is local only

The SOS screen can flash the screen, play an optional alarm, and use the torch if the phone allows it. It does not place a call and does not contact emergency services.

Family medical card

Store blood type, allergies, and other notes on the device. There is no account and no SafeNow server for this card.

Supply tracker

A supplies list you keep on the phone, so you can see what you already have on hand.

Battery saver

A mode that switches the app to a darker screen, to reduce what the display is doing.

Readiness check

A checklist that shows whether guides are in the app, whether a real shelter is saved, and whether a family plan has been started. It is a reminder on this phone, not a certification that the app has been tested on a physical device in airplane mode.

Phrase cards

Short cards for asking for help, water, a doctor, shelter, and family, in the language you choose.

Optional walking map

If you want a small street map or walking streets, save an area while you are online. Map tiles and nearby OpenStreetMap places are fetched only when you ask. Community map data can be incomplete. Sample places are marked SAMPLE and are not official shelters.

How it is designed to work offline

Open the app once with a connection. After that first launch, the core tools are designed to stay on the phone. This has not been published as a test on a physical device in airplane mode.

  1. Open it once online

    Connect, open SafeNow, and let the bundled guides and your settings finish loading. The app cannot save itself if the first launch has no network.

  2. Keep the guides on the phone

    After first launch, the compass, your saved places, the checklists, and the bundled guides are meant to stay available without internet, Wi-Fi, or cell service. GPS can still work from satellites.

  3. Save a map only if you want one

    A street map is optional. If you want one later, save a small area while you are online. Map tiles are © OpenStreetMap contributors and are only fetched when you ask.

Safety disclaimer

The SOS screen is a light and sound on this phone only. SafeNow does not place a call and does not contact emergency services. If you are in danger, use the official emergency number for the area you are in.

SafeNow does not replace official warnings. If authorities tell you to do something else, do that.

Sample places are marked SAMPLE and are not official shelters. Community map data from OpenStreetMap can be incomplete. Load a real list from your community before a crisis when you can.

Guide text in languages other than English was translated with machine assistance and still needs review by a native speaker. First-aid pages are reference guidance, not a substitute for training or professional care.

Questions

Does SafeNow contact emergency services?

No. The SOS screen is a light and sound on this phone only. It does not place a call and it does not contact emergency services. If you are in danger, use the official emergency number for the area you are in.

Does it work offline?

SafeNow is designed to work offline after first launch. After you open it once with a connection, the compass, your saved places, the checklists, and the bundled guides are meant to stay available without internet, Wi-Fi, or cell service. GPS can still work from satellites. A street map is optional and is only fetched when you ask, while you are online.

Is SafeNow free?

This version is free and does not take payment. The planned model is free in regions with an active emergency and $1 a month elsewhere. Safety features would never be paywalled. That plan is not live, and billing is not built yet.

When will it be on the App Store and Google Play?

Coming soon to the App Store and Google Play. SafeNow has been submitted for App Store review and is not yet available to download on either store.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no account and no login. Places, family notes, medical card details, and checklist ticks stay on the device. See the privacy policy.

Where does the guidance come from?

Guidance is drawn from Ready.gov, FEMA, CDC, the American Red Cross, and public-domain U.S. Army survival manuals. Map data is © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. SafeNow does not replace official warnings.

Are the translations finished?

The app is available in 20 languages. Guide text in languages other than English was translated with machine assistance and still needs review by a native speaker before a community relies on it. First-aid action steps stay in English so a translation cannot change them.

What are the SAMPLE places?

Sample places are fictional and marked SAMPLE. They are not official shelters. Community OpenStreetMap data can be incomplete. Save real places or an official list for your area while you still have a connection, when you can.

How do I get help with the app?

If you are in danger now, use the official emergency number for the area you are in. For help with the app itself, see the support page.
