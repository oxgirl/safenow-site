Privacy policy

Last updated 25 November 2026. This policy covers the SafeNow iOS and Android apps, the SafeNow Maps iOS app (see SafeNow Maps), and this website.

No account, no analytics

SafeNow has no account and no login. It does not include an analytics, advertising, crash, or tracking SDK. It does not sell personal information. There is no SafeNow server that receives your places, family notes, medical card, checklist ticks, or settings. The only data SafeNow itself receives is described under Optional alert notifications below, and only if you turn them on.

What stays on the device

Places, routes you save, the household plan and family medical card, checklist ticks, language, and settings are stored on the device (IndexedDB and local storage). Uninstalling the app or clearing the site data removes them. You can export a JSON or CSV file yourself if you want a copy.

Location, compass, and camera

SafeNow uses your location for the map, directions, the compass and SOS. Your location stays on this phone. SafeNow shares it only when you choose to, such as in an SOS text you send yourself or when you download map data for the area around you. A last-known fix can be stored on the device so the compass can keep working if the GPS signal drops. SafeNow itself does not receive your location unless you turn on optional alert notifications, described below. SafeNow does not track your location in the background.

The compass uses motion sensors so the arrow can turn with the phone. Those readings stay on the device. SafeNow uses the camera only while you scan a QR code from another phone, such as a shared pin or a move-to-a-new-phone code. Nothing is recorded or sent.

If you deny location, you can still type a latitude and longitude or search for a city name.

Optional alert notifications

Notifications about official warnings while SafeNow is closed are off unless you turn them on in the app. When they are on, the app sends the SafeNow alert relay a push notification token, your language, and coarse locations rounded to about 1 km: your current location and up to nine places you saved, without their names. The relay uses this only to send notifications about official warnings from the U.S. National Weather Service, the NOAA tsunami warning centers, and the U.S. Geological Survey for those areas. It is not linked to your identity, there is no account, and it is not used for tracking or advertising. Turning the notifications or alerts off removes your subscription from the relay, and a subscription from a phone that stops checking in is deleted after 60 days. A record of which notifications were sent is kept for up to 14 days. Notifications can be late or may not arrive, so keep your phone’s emergency alerts turned on. Emergency audio recordings stay on your phone and are never sent to SafeNow.

Network requests you can start while online

The app can work from data already on the phone. It does not need a network for the bundled guides, translations, or places you have already saved. If you choose to search for a city, update nearby shelter data, or save a walking-route or street-map area while you have a connection, the app sends that request to public OpenStreetMap services, not to a SafeNow server:

  • A place-name search or reverse-geocode request to Nominatim (nominatim.openstreetmap.org). That request includes the search text you typed, or coordinates if you asked the app to name a point.
  • A bounded map query to the public Overpass API (overpass-api.de) so OpenStreetMap places or walking streets in that area can be saved on the phone. That request includes a geographic bounding box.
  • Map tiles from OpenStreetMap (tile.openstreetmap.org) only if you ask to save or view a street map. Tile requests indicate the map area you viewed.

Those services can see the request and the network address it came from, under their own policies. SafeNow does not receive a copy. Official shelter lists included with the app are read from files on the device, not from a SafeNow backend. Map data is © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.

Some guide screens include ordinary links to public references such as Ready.gov, the American Red Cross, CDC, or WHO. Those pages load only if you open the link.

SafeNow Maps

SafeNow Maps (app.safenow.maps) is a separate iOS map app. It does not collect data. It has no account or login, and it does not include an analytics, advertising, crash, or tracking SDK. Saved maps, saved places, and settings are stored on the device and are removed when you delete them or uninstall the app.

SafeNow Maps uses your location only while you use the app, to show your position on the map, measure distances, and give walking and driving directions. Directions are calculated on the phone from map data on the device. Your location stays on the device. It is not sent to SafeNow or to anyone else, and the app does not track your location in the background. If you deny location, you can still search the map and choose start and end points yourself. A place or your location is shared only when you tap Share, which opens the iOS share sheet. You choose the recipient, and the app does not send anything itself.

SafeNow Maps makes the network requests below. None of them includes your position, an account, or a device identifier:

  • Map files. When you save an area, or view the map while you have a connection, the app downloads map data for that area from the SafeNow map file server (safenow-maps.safenow.workers.dev, hosted on Cloudflare). The request shows which map area is downloaded, which can be the area around you, but not your exact position. The server only serves files, and SafeNow keeps no logs of who downloads what. Cloudflare, as the host, may process standard network data such as IP addresses under its own privacy policy.
  • Road closures. The app can read public closure feeds published by transportation agencies, such as Caltrans (cwwp2.dot.ca.gov) and state work-zone (WZDx) feeds, chosen for the region of a saved map.
  • Hazard zones. The app can request official hazard-zone layers, such as CAL FIRE fire hazard severity zones, California tsunami hazard areas, and U.S. Geological Survey fault layers, for the bounding box of a saved map area.
  • Place details. If you ask for more details about a place on the map, the app can query the public OpenStreetMap Overpass API (overpass-api.de) with that place’s coordinates.

These services can see the request and the network address it came from, under their own policies. SafeNow does not receive a copy. Map data is © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.

This website

These pages are static files hosted on GitHub Pages and served at safenow.help. This site does not set a tracking cookie, does not include an analytics script, and does not use an account. GitHub, as the host, may process standard web-server logs such as IP addresses under GitHub’s own privacy policy.

Payments

This version does not take payment and does not include a store billing account or in-app purchase.
